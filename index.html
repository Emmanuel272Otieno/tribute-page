Who Was Nelson Mandela?

Nelson Rolihlahla Mandela was born on 18 July 1918 in Mvezo, South Africa. Known affectionately as Madiba, he became one of the world's most respected leaders in the fight against apartheid, a system of racial segregation and discrimination.

Mandela studied law and became involved in the African National Congress (ANC). He worked for a democratic society where people could enjoy equal rights regardless of race.

His struggle for freedom led to more than 27 years in prison. After his release in 1990, he helped negotiate the end of apartheid and encouraged reconciliation among South Africans.

His Life Through Time

Explore the milestones that shaped his journey.

1918

A New Life Begins

Mandela was born in Mvezo, in South Africa's Eastern Cape, on 18 July.

1944

Joined the Freedom Struggle

He helped establish the ANC Youth League, working for political and social change.

1964

Sentenced to Life Imprisonment

During the Rivonia Trial, Mandela was sentenced to life imprisonment for his role in the struggle against apartheid.

1990

Freedom at Last

He was released from prison on 11 February after more than 27 years in captivity.

1993

Nobel Peace Prize

Mandela and F. W. de Klerk received the Nobel Peace Prize for their work toward peacefully ending apartheid.

1994

Made History

Mandela became South Africa's first democratically elected president on 10 May 1994.

2013

A Lasting Legacy

Mandela died on 5 December, aged 95. His example continues to inspire people around the world.
